What is maximum CAC?

It’s the most you can pay to acquire a customer without losing money, also called break-even CAC. It equals the contribution margin that customer leaves you across all their orders: pay more and every new customer loses you money; pay less and the gap is your profit.

How is it calculated?

Multiply the contribution margin of each order by the number of orders you expect each customer to place.

Margin per order = average order value − variable costs

Break-even CAC = margin per order × orders per customer

Target CAC = break-even CAC − profit per customer

Orders to pay back CAC = CAC ÷ margin per order

With the calculator’s example, 60 − 24 − 6 − 1.80 − 7.20 = 21 per order, or 35%, and 21 × 1.5 = 31.50. If you want to make 10 per customer, your target CAC is 21.50.

The first order pays back 21; the rest depends on orders that arrive months later. If your cash flow can’t wait that long, the CAC you can afford is the one the first order pays back, even if break-even sits higher.

How many orders per customer should I enter?

The number you’ve actually measured in your store. Take the customers who placed their first order in a given period, count how many orders they placed over the next 12 months and divide orders by customers. With only a few months of data, don’t add purchases you can’t be sure of. If you sell something people buy once every several years, enter 1.

Why margin and not revenue?

Because revenue counts as available money that has already gone on product, shipping and fees. A customer who places 1.5 orders of 60 brings in 90 of revenue but leaves only 31.50 of margin, so any rule applied to the 90 lets you pay almost three times too much. Fixed costs such as salaries or rent stay out; they’re covered by the gap between your CAC and break-even.

Compare the result with your paid CAC: campaign spend divided by the new customers those campaigns brought in. If you give a welcome coupon, count it only once, either off first-order margin or added to CAC.

How do I raise the CAC I can afford?

There are three ways: more margin per order, more orders per customer, or customers who bring in other customers. The third is how Tuko works, our group buying app for Shopify: the group price only applies if someone else joins through the first buyer’s link, so the discount works as the cost of acquiring the second customer. It’s on the Shopify App Store.
