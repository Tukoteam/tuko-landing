What is break-even ROAS?

It’s the ROAS at which a campaign neither makes nor loses money: the margin from attributed sales exactly covers what you spend on ads. Below that figure, each dollar or euro you spend on ads brings back less margin than it costs.

How is it calculated?

Divide 1 by your contribution margin. If you apply a discount, the margin left is smaller and break-even goes up.

Margin (m) = (price − variable costs) ÷ price

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ m

With a discount d = (1 − d) ÷ (m − d)

With the calculator’s example, (50 − 30) ÷ 50 = 40% and 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.5. With a 20% discount, (1 − 0.20) ÷ (0.40 − 0.20) = 4.0. If the discount equals or exceeds the margin, no ROAS covers the campaign.

With your current ROAS, what you make or lose per 100 of ad spend is 100 × (ROAS × margin − 1), with the margin measured on the price the customer pays. Costs count as fixed amounts per order, so with a discount the result is slightly conservative.

Why contribution margin and not gross margin?

Because gross margin only subtracts the product cost, while every sale also costs you shipping, payment fees, packaging and returns. With gross margin, break-even comes out lower than it really is and campaigns that lose money look fine. Fixed costs such as salaries or rent stay out.

Prices go in excluding sales tax/VAT, because the tax you collect isn’t your margin. Before comparing with the ROAS in Google Ads or Meta Ads, check whether the conversion value they receive includes tax.

What if a discount pushes break-even too high?

You have three options: discount less, limit the discount to fewer products, or only give it to customers who bring someone else. The third is how Tuko works, our group buying app for Shopify: the lower price only applies if another customer joins, so the discount doesn’t come out of every sale. It’s on the Shopify App Store.
