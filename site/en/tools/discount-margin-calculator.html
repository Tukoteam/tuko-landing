How many more orders do you need with a discount?

Divide what a full-price order leaves you by what a discounted one leaves you. If a regular order leaves you three times what a discounted one does, you need three times as many discounted orders to earn the same.

How is it calculated?

With contribution per order: what’s left of the price after every cost that comes with a sale. Fees go in as a percentage because they shrink with the price; the other costs go in as amounts per order.

Contribution = price × (1 − fees) − costs per order − ads

Orders needed = contribution without discount ÷ contribution with discount

Minimum price = (costs per order + ads) ÷ (1 − fees)

Maximum discount = 1 − (minimum price ÷ price)

With the calculator’s example, 60 × 0.95 − 35 = 22 at full price. With a 20% discount, 48 × 0.95 − 35 = 10.60, and 22 ÷ 10.60 = 2.08: for every 100 regular orders you need 208 discounted ones. With 5 of ads per order, the discounted order leaves 5.60 and you need 3.93 times the orders. Without ads, the minimum price is 35 ÷ 0.95 = 36.84, a maximum discount of 38.6%.

Full-price orders are assumed to carry no ad cost; if yours do, subtract it from the full-price contribution too. Use the cost per order you expect during the promotion, not a quiet month’s.

Why isn’t gross margin enough?

Because the discount comes out of what’s left after shipping, packaging, returns and fees, not out of price minus product cost. In the example, the product looks like it has 35 of margin but leaves only 22, so a 25% discount takes 15 of price out of those 22.

What about customers who would have bought anyway?

They cost you too. Every discounted order that would have come in at full price costs you the gap between the two contributions, 11.40 in the 20% example. The promotion pays off when new orders times discounted contribution cover cannibalized orders times that gap, plus what you spend on ads.

That’s why the shape of the discount matters as much as its size. A sitewide discount also goes to people who were going to buy anyway. With Tuko, our group buying app for Shopify, the lower price only applies if one more person joins, so nobody gets it without bringing another customer. It’s on the Shopify App Store.
