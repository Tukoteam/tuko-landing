A Tuko campaign, start to finish

Two minutes: how you set up the campaign, what your customer sees on the product page, and what happens when the goal is hit.

The problem

Cutting the price isn't the problem.
Giving it away to people who were
going to buy anyway is.

Every flat discount also goes to customers who would have bought at full price. You're paying margin on sales you already had.

01

Every new customer costs you more

The cost of acquiring a customer in ecommerce has risen more than 200% in a decade. Every sale you bring in with ads is more expensive than the last.

02

Idle stock eats your margin

Cutting 30% moves it, sure. But it wipes the profit on the whole batch—and customers who were already going to buy take the discount too.

03

A flat discount doesn't pay for itself

A −20% on a product with 60% margin forces you to sell 67% more units just to stay even. That almost never happens.

How it works

A discount with conditions.
Yours.

You pick a product, a unit goal and a deadline. The discount doesn't exist until the goal is met. Everything happens inside your store: the customer never leaves your site.

01

You pick a product and a goal

One product, a unit goal and a deadline (72 hours works well). You set the discount ladder: you never give away a point of margin beyond what you decide.

02

Your customers reserve, as usual

They see on the product page how many units are left for the next price and go through your usual checkout—no signing up elsewhere, no meeting anyone. The amount is held on their card, but nothing is charged yet.

03

They bring the ones still missing

Anyone who already reserved wants the goal to be hit, so they share it. That traffic didn't cost you a thing.

04

The campaign closes and everyone is charged

When the deadline ends, everyone is charged the price of the tier reached. If none is reached, each customer pays the regular price and the sale is still yours. You never lose a sale for trying.
